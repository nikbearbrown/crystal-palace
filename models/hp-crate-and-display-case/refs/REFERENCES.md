# REFERENCES — hp-crate-and-display-case

All images are copies from this repository (`nikbearbrown/crystal-palace`,
branch `main`, folder `references/muse-drive/images/commons/`), taken
2026-10-26. Licences are the round-1 manifest's
(`references/muse-drive/data/manifest.csv`).

## Used for the display case's form

- `Cotton,_carriages,_etc_-_Dickinson's_comprehensive_pictures_of_the_Great_Exhibition_(1854)_vol_2,_plate_XXIV_-_BL.jpg`
  — Dickinson's Comprehensive Pictures, vol. 2, plate XXIV ("Cotton,
  carriages, etc"), British Library copy. **CC0.**
  The controlling source. The Cotton court shows the standard 1851
  table case three times: left (two men examining contents), centre
  (a pair, with a child) and mid-distance. Read from it: the timber
  table frame on turned legs, the deep apron under the lid, the
  shallow hipped glazed lid with a centre mullion on the long slopes,
  exhibits lying flat on the bed. Overall size is **guessed** from
  these views by proportion against the standing figures — no
  dimensioned source exists (see NOTES.md).
- `crop_xxiv_cases_left.png`, `crop_xxiv_cases_centre.png` —
  enlargements of the plate-XXIV cases, made from the file above for
  this build. Same CC0 source. The centre crop is where the turned
  leg profile and the lid's hip/mullion pattern were read.
- `crop_xxiv_cabinet_right.png` — enlargement of the large exhibitor's
  cabinet at the right of plate XXIV (fluted corner columns,
  pediment, gilt frieze). **Checked and deliberately NOT modelled:**
  it is a one-off exhibitor showpiece, not the standard case, and
  copying its ornament would invent a specificity the scenes don't
  need. Recorded here so the choice is auditable.

## Corroboration / context (form checked, not dimensioned from)

- `India_No.4_-_Dickinson's_comprehensive_pictures_of_the_Great_Exhibition_(1854)_vol_2,_plate_V_-_BL.jpg`
  — plate V, India No. 4. **CC0.** A timber-framed glazed cabinet
  with a shallow pitched glazed top stands in the mid-background
  (right of the elephant): same family of case furniture. Most India
  exhibits sit on open draped plinths, which is why CP025's jewel
  display is served by the standard glazed case, empty — the actual
  Koh-i-Noor case design is **not** evidenced here and was not
  invented.
- `Moving_Machinery_-_Dickinson's_comprehensive_pictures_of_the_Great_Exhibition_(1854)_vol_2,_plate_XXI_-_Royal_Coll.jpg`
  — plate XXI, Moving Machinery. **Public domain.** The machinery
  court works by open machines on plinths, a long glazed counter
  case (left, "Arbroath Manufactures") and barriers — the context
  for CP024's "case/barrier, not the machine itself".
- `Guernsey,_Jersey,_Malta,_Ceylon_-_Dickinson's_comprehensive_pictures_of_the_Great_Exhibition_(1854)_vol_2,_plate_X_-_BL.jpg`
  — plate X. **CC0.** Small exhibits under glass domes on draped
  tables, and a small glazed booth cabinet (centre, "Ionian Isles").
  Checked: the domes are a different display device (not modelled).

## Checked, no usable case/crate evidence

- `nash_plate2_foreign_nave.jpg` — Joseph Nash, The Great Industrial
  Exhibition of 1851, plate 2, The Foreign Nave. **Public domain.**
  A crowd panorama; no case or crate is legible at modelling scale.
- The round-1 manifest (`references/muse-drive/data/manifest.csv`,
  8,860 catalogue rows) was searched for case / vitrine / crate /
  packing / jewel descriptions. The only case hit is plate XXIV
  itself ("cases of crepe, muslin and cotton goods"). **No image of
  a packing crate exists in the repo references** — the crate in
  this prop is generic period construction, labelled as such in
  NOTES.md; no rope handles, iron corners or stencilled lettering
  were added, because nothing in the references evidences them.
