# Candidates — prop models mined from the reference collection

Mined by Muse from `references/muse-drive/images/` (1,398 files actually present in the repo, per
`references/muse-drive/data/manifest.csv`, status `downloaded`), plus `references/downes-1852/`
(Downes 1852 working drawings, with plate subjects confirmed against
`references/downes-1852/crystal-palace-1851/PLATE-INDEX.md`) and `references/image-leads/`.

The top candidates were checked by eye against the actual images, not just filenames. Views are
described as seen. **Feasibility is recorded honestly but is NOT a build order** — per Bear's
amendment, models are built in random order because other bots draw from the same pool.

Feasibility key: **High** = strong reference(s), mostly procedural/lathe work, few guesses ·
**Medium** = buildable, but a view is missing or organic sculpting is involved · **Low** = only
distant, partial or single-aspect references; expect heavy inference (mark it as such in NOTES.md).

## Famous objects with NO usable reference in the collection

Searched the full downloaded manifest (title + description) and the leads; reporting the gaps so
nobody else wastes a pass:

- **The Greek Slave (Hiram Powers)** — zero hits. The single most famous statue of 1851 is not
  pictured anywhere in the 1,398 downloaded files.
- **Koh-i-Noor diamond and its display case/cage** — zero hits for the diamond or its case.
- **The Amazon on horseback (August Kiss)** — no dedicated image. The gilt equestrian group is
  visible only at a distance, mid-transept, in Nash's Plate 4
  (`references/muse-drive/images/commons/The_great_industrial_exhibition_of_1851._Plate_4._The_transept_LCCN2003665159.jpg`)
  and as a small equestrian figure in the Wellcome engraving at #12 — not enough to model from.
- **Sydenham sphinxes** — the only sphinx file, `CPsphinxsteps02.jpg`, is a 1980s photo of the
  empty concrete base and steps; the sphinx itself is not shown.
- **Benches, lamps, chairs as standalone subjects** — furniture appears only incidentally in wide
  views (see #37, #38). No lamp standard is photographed close anywhere in the downloaded set.

## Method notes (read before building)

- The book-scan sections (`tallis-history-v1/v4`, `routledge-1851-guide`,
  `downes-1852-working-drawings`, the 1856 Sydenham guide pages) are page-numbered scans with
  generic manifest descriptions, so objects inside them are **not** individually indexed here.
  The Routledge guide's plates and Tallis's engravings certainly contain more exhibits; a
  page-by-page plate pass over those ~880 pages is the obvious next mining step.
- The `new-york-crystal-palace` section (114 files) was excluded: a different building.
- Two filename traps found while eyeballing: `The_Crystal_Palace_page_52a.jpg` is an engraving of
  a chain-making machine, **not** the column base its neighbours suggest; and
  `Donald_McLeish_Glass.webp`, described as "glass and girders", is in fact a Sydenham exterior
  photograph (used at #39).
- Licences above are as recorded in the manifest for the key reference of each row; check the
  manifest row for any file before reusing it outside the course, per the repo README.
