# References — chatsworth-great-stove-rib-bay

The Great Stove (Great Conservatory), Chatsworth, Derbyshire, 1836–40,
by Joseph Paxton (with Decimus Burton associated with the design in some
accounts). Demolished 1920. Nothing of it survives above ground, and the
project's own image collection is thin on it — the one Great Conservatory
item in the round-1 manifest (`File:Chatsworth - Great Conservatory in
the 19th century.JPG`) was catalogued URL-only (status `skipped-budget`)
and is not on repo main. The dimensioned evidence below is therefore the
period photograph in the round-2 collection plus period printed
descriptions found by web search, each cited for exactly what was taken
from it. No web images were downloaded into this folder; the only image
copies here are from the project's own collection.

## Image reference (geometry form)

- **`great-conservatory-19c-photo.jpg`** — 19th-century photograph of the
  Great Conservatory, 1035×611. Public domain (Wikimedia Commons,
  `File:Chatsworth - Great Conservatory in the 19th century.JPG`).
  Project copies: round-2 collection, branch `muse-round2`,
  `references/muse-round2/images/S03-great-conservatory-chatsworth-19c-photo.jpg`;
  also catalogued in `references/muse-drive/data/manifest.csv`.
  Taken from it: the three-tier stepped section (high central vessel,
  lower curved side roofs stepping down to a masonry base); the central
  profile — steep low down, flattening to a broad shallow crown, **not**
  a semicircle; side roofs springing from a valley ledge above the
  aisles; the fine transverse rib/ridge-and-furrow striping on all
  tiers; the diagonal-lattice ventilator band at the foot of the side
  glazing; the rusticated stone plinth with wide shallow round arches
  between piers; the small classical end portico (seen, not modelled —
  it belongs to the end wall, not a rib bay).
- **`crop-end-profile.png`** — detail crop of the same photograph (end
  profile): confirms the tiers step down with curved (hipped) ends, the
  valley ledge, and stone corbels under the side rib feet.
- **`crop-base-arches.png`** — detail crop of the same photograph (base):
  the ventilator lattice panels between the rib feet and the plinth's
  shallow arched recesses between rusticated piers.

## Printed dimensions (period sources, web search 2026-10-26)

Three period or near-period descriptions agree on the figures used:

- **Morris, *Picturesque Views of the Seats of Noblemen and Gentlemen
  of Great Britain and Ireland*, pp. 49–52** (read at fromoldbooks.org /
  c82.net mirrors): plan **276 × 123 ft**; "its centre arched roof,
  **sixty-seven feet high**, with a span of **seventy feet**, resting on
  **two rows of iron pillars, twenty-eight feet high**"; upwards of
  70,000 sq ft of glass; eight underground boilers.
- ***Black's Guide* (1864)**, quoted at andrewsgen.com: same figures
  (prints length 277 ft).
- **Cokayne, *A Day in the Peak* (1889)** (texts.wishful-thinking.org.uk):
  same figures.

Taken from these: overall width 123 ft, central span 70 ft, crown
67 ft, pillar/springing height 28 ft. Length (276/277 ft) is printed
but unused — this prop is a single transverse bay slice.

## Construction (web source)

- **Paxton biography, assignmentpoint.com** (summarising the standard
  account): "The curved ridge-and-furrow glazed timber roof was carried
  on **arched laminated-timber frames supported on cast-iron columns**
  and buttressed by the side arches over the flanking aisles"; columns
  and beams cast iron, arched elements laminated wood. Taken from it:
  the material split modelled here — laminated-timber arch ribs,
  cast-iron pillars, lighter side arches.

## Conflicting figures (recorded, not used)

- **chatsworth.org (official site, Great Conservatory page)**: 84 m
  long, 37 m wide, **19 m high** (≈ 62 ft). Length and width agree with
  the period figures (276 ft ≈ 84 m; 123 ft ≈ 37.5 m); the modern
  height does not — the period 67 ft (20.42 m) is adopted because three
  period descriptions agree on it and the photograph's proportions
  (crown height against the 123 ft width and 70 ft span) fit it better.
- One secondary web account gives the length as **227 ft** against the
  period 276/277 ft and the official 84 m; treated as a transcription
  error, unused either way.
- **Trap avoided:** Loudon's *Encyclopaedia of Gardening* §2077 (via
  gardenvisit.com) quotes Paxton's ridge-and-furrow method for a
  **smaller, earlier Chatsworth house** (97½ × 26 ft, pillars 6½ ft
  apart) — not the Great Stove. None of its dimensions were used here.

## Deliberately excluded

- The Victoria Regia / Lily House images in the round-1 manifest are a

  Stove evidence.
- The existing package `models/chatsworth-glasshouse-rib-bay/` (the
  parallel builder's illustrative version, all dimensions guessed) was
  read only to avoid duplicating it — see the Relationship section of
