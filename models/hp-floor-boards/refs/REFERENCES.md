# References — hp-floor-boards

All evidence is already inside the project repo `nikbearbrown/crystal-palace`.
Local copies of the three page images sit in this folder; the Downes
documents were read in the repo and are listed by path (not copied).

## Copied into this folder

### 1. `S25-01-official-catalogue-1851-introduction-p62-flooring.jpg`
- **Repo path:** `references/muse-round2/images/S25-01-official-catalogue-1851-introduction-p62-flooring.jpg`
  (branch **`muse-round2`** — the round-2 collection; not yet on `main`)
- **What it is:** *Official Descriptive and Illustrated Catalogue of the
  Great Exhibition* (1851), Introduction, printed p. 62, section
  "Construction of the Building — The flooring". It quotes Paxton's own
  account (from his Society of Arts paper of 13 November 1850):
  > "The boards for the floor are 1½ inch thick, laid half an inch
  > apart, upon joists 7 inches by 2½ inches, which rest upon large
  > timbers or sleepers, 13 inches by 3¼ inches, at intervals of
  > 8 feet apart. Through the interstices left between the boards
  > the dust passes…"
  This page is the primary source for every structural dimension of
  the model: board thickness, gap, joist section, sleeper section,
  sleeper spacing.
- **Licence:** Public domain (1851 publication; Internet Archive scan),
  as recorded in the round-2 manifest.

### 2. `S25-02-official-catalogue-1851-introduction-p63-flooring-conclusion.jpg`
- **Repo path:** `references/muse-round2/images/S25-02-official-catalogue-1851-introduction-p63-flooring-conclusion.jpg`
  (branch **`muse-round2`**)
- **What it is:** the conclusion of the same passage, printed p. 63:
  "thoroughly washed, the water at once disappears between the
  openings, and the boards become almost immediately fit for
  visitors" — the washing behaviour the open gaps exist for — and the
  context figure "the total area of the ground floor is 772,784
  square feet".
- **Licence:** Public domain, as above.

### 3. `S25-berlyn-1851-floor-description.jpg`
- **Repo path:** `references/muse-round2/images/S25-berlyn-1851-floor-description.jpg`
  (branch **`muse-round2`**)
- **What it is:** Berlyn & Fowler, *The Crystal Palace* (1851), p. 37.
  Independent corroboration of the gap, and the crucial contrast with
  the gallery floor:
  > "The whole of the floor is boarded; that below is laid with an
  > interval of half an inch between the boards, to allow the passage
  > of dust from the millions of feet by which it will be trod; the
  > gallery floor, on the contrary, has iron tongues between the
  > boards to prevent the dust from coming through on the heads of
  > the visitors below."
- **Licence:** Public domain (1851 publication).

## Read in the repo, not copied (branch `main`)

- `references/downes-1852/crystal-palace-1851/CONTROL-DIMENSIONS.md` —
  checked for floor controls. It dimensions floor *levels* (e.g. D12,
  gallery floor 22 ft 11 in above ground floor) but contains **no**
  dimensions for the ground-floor boards, joists or sleepers; the
  Catalogue text above is the only dimensioned source for the members.
- `references/downes-1852/crystal-palace-1851/MEASUREMENTS.md` —
  Plate VI (transverse section) measurement notes: floor line at the
  column feet, with "the ground below the boards" as the next line
  down, ≈33 px ≈ 1⅓ ft at the plate's scale. Used only as a loose
  cross-check on the underfloor depth (see NOTES.md) — the plate is a
  whole-building section and is not dimensioned for the floor build-up.
- `references/downes-1852/crystal-palace-1851/PLATE-INDEX.md` —
  plate identification (Plate I ground-floor plan; Plate VI section).

## Deliberately NOT used

- The round-1 item known as **"Testing the Gallery Floor"** (and the
  Berlyn gallery-floor-test page, `S25-berlyn-1851-gallery-floor-test.jpg`
  on `muse-round2`) shows the **gallery** floor — a different
  construction (boards with iron tongues between them, per Berlyn
  p. 37 above). It is not evidence for this ground-floor prop and no
  part of the model is taken from it.
- No Sydenham (1854) source of any kind: this prop is Hyde Park 1851
  only.
