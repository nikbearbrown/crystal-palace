# REFERENCES — sy-gothic-arch-fragment

All sources are from the repo's own round-1 collection
(`references/muse-drive/`), copied here from the identical local
mirror of that collection. Licences are the round-1 manifest's
(`references/muse-drive/data/manifest.csv`). No source outside the
repo collection was used for geometry. Round 2
(`references/muse-round2/`, branch `muse-round2`) was also checked:
its only court photograph is the Roman Court (S57-02) — a different
court, not used here.

## Primary sources (form of the bay)

- `guide-1856-p89-guisborough-arcade.jpg` — 1856 *Guide to the
  Crystal Palace and Park* (Samuel Phillips), printed p.89. Text:
  the English Mediæval Court has "a cloister of the Decorated
  period, founded in its arches and columns on the Abbey of
  Guisborough, Yorkshire"; the page carries the captioned woodcut
  **"Arcade from Guisborough"** — a two-light bay: two tall pointed
  lights under one moulded containing arch, a quatrefoil panel
  centred in the spandrel, and a frieze band of square quatrefoil
  panels above. This woodcut is the prop's form source. Public
  domain (manifest). Repo path:
  `references/muse-drive/images/commons/Guide_to_the_Crystal_Palace_and_Park_1856_(147900476).jpg`.
  Enlarged crop: `crop-guisborough-arcade.png`.
- `guide-1856-p92-cloister-elevation.jpg` — same guide, printed
  p.92, captioned **"Elevation of English Medieval Court towards
  the cloister"**: the long elevation of the same cloister front —
  groups of four small arches under pairs of containing arches,
  trefoil and quatrefoil spandrel panels, statue niches at the
  piers (statues NOT modelled — figurative sculpture is excluded
  from this project), a sculptured tympanum band, a running
  quatrefoil frieze, crenellated parapet and crocketed pinnacles.
  Used to confirm the bay rhythm and the relative height of the
  upper band. Public domain (manifest). Repo path:
  `.../Guide_to_the_Crystal_Palace_and_Park_1856_(147900480).jpg`.
  Enlarged crop: `crop-cloister-elevation.png`.

## Supporting sources

- `guide-1856-p86-english-mediaeval-entrance.jpg` — same guide,
  printed p.86, captioned woodcut **"Entrance to English Mediæval
  Court"**: a great portal whose jambs are **clustered shafts** of
  several orders with small capitals at differing heights — the
  evidence for building this prop's piers as clusters rather than
  single shafts — flanked by blind arches with quatrefoil circles,
  on a moulded plinth. Public domain (manifest). Repo path:
  `.../Guide_to_the_Crystal_Palace_and_Park_1856_(147900472).jpg`.
- `medieval-vestibule-photo-rp-f-2012-96-334.jpg` — Rijksmuseum
  stereoscopic photograph, "Crystal Palace. 4. The French and
  Italian Medieval Vestibule" (the vestibule off the nave whose
  wall arches were copied from the choir of Notre-Dame, Paris —
  guide p.94). A dated photograph of Sydenham medieval-court
  fabric: great pointed arches with moulded orders springing from
  columns whose shafts read as clusters of a main roll with an
  attached smaller roll, **dark polished shafts under pale, deeply
  carved foliate bell capitals with round abaci** (the tonal
  contrast the model's two materials follow), a freestanding dark
  column on a moulded circular base and stepped round plinth, and
  a quatrefoil roundel in the arch spandrel. Used for capital
  character, shaft material and as the scale anchor (see
  NOTES.md). CC0 (manifest). Repo path:
  `references/muse-drive/images/commons/Beeldhouwwerken_in_de_Franse_en_Italiaanse_Middeleeuwse_voorhal_van_Crystal_Palace_in_Sydenham_Crystal_Palace._4._The_French_and_Italian_Medieval_Vestibule._(titel_op_object),_RP-F-2012-96-334.jpg`.
  Enlarged crop: `crop-vestibule-arch-photo.png`.
- `guide-1856-p94-french-italian-vestibule-plan.jpg` — same guide,
  printed p.94: "THE FRENCH AND ITALIAN MEDIÆVAL VESTIBULE" with
  its ground plan — establishes that the photographed vestibule is
  a distinct small space off the nave, separate from the English
  Mediæval Court; used to keep the two spaces' evidence separate,
  exactly as the guide keeps them separate. Public domain
  (manifest). Repo path:
  `.../Guide_to_the_Crystal_Palace_and_Park_1856_(147900483).jpg`.

## Text evidence (same guide, read at full resolution)

- p.89: the cloister is "of the Decorated period, founded in its
  arches and columns on the Abbey of Guisborough, Yorkshire"; the
  court's upper portion has arches with angel figures in the
  spandrels from the "Angel Choir" of Lincoln Cathedral; the
  tympanum sculptures are mostly from Tewkesbury; the figures
  under the canopies from Wells Cathedral; the great doorway
  facing the nave is from Rochester Cathedral, "coloured"; the
  practice of colouring and gilding "was carried to an almost
  extravagant extent in the Gothic style".
- p.92 (text around the elevation): the walls round the court are
  formed of portions from Southwell Minster, Ely Cathedral,
  Beverley Minster and Lichfield.
- p.86–95 generally: the English Mediæval Court is one of the
  sequence of national Mediæval courts (German, English, French
  and Italian) — separate spaces. The storyboard's literal court
  sequence is unconfirmed (SCENE-BUILD-PLAN item 13); this prop
  asserts nothing about adjacency.

## Checked and NOT used

- "Mediaeval Court from the Great Exhibition of 1851" (MET
  DP258011, round-1 collection, full and cropped versions) —
  Pugin's Mediaeval Court at **Hyde Park, 1851**, a different
  building and a different design. Excluded under the
  Sydenham-only rule; not used for any dimension or detail.
- Delamotte photographs: no view of any Sydenham Mediæval court is
  in the round-1 collection (searched by filename and manifest);
  the vestibule photograph above (an anonymous stereoscopic view,
  Rijksmuseum RP-F-2012-96-334) is the only dated photograph of
  Sydenham medieval-court fabric in the collection.
- The Italian Court stereograph (round 1,
  `images/commons/CrystalPalaceItalianCourt.jpg`) — the
  Renaissance Court of SCENE-BUILD-PLAN item 13's second fragment.
  Evidence for it exists, but that fragment is a separate build
  and is NOT part of this prop; see NOTES.md.
