# NOTES — Gothic arch fragment (English Mediæval Court cloister bay)

Prop for the Crystal Palace film (SCENE-BUILD-PLAN Wave 3, item 13).
Scene: CP040 (walking through arranged history — the plan pairs a
Gothic detail with a Renaissance fragment in two separately
established court spaces; see "Scope" below).
**Sydenham 1854 only** — no Hyde Park evidence is used in this model.

## The interpretation caveat (read first)

**This is a Victorian reproduction, not medieval Gothic.** The
English Mediæval Court was one of the Fine Arts Courts built at
Sydenham in 1854 under the direction of Matthew Digby Wyatt (with
Owen Jones directing the courts' decoration) — a didactic,
plaster-and-timber arrangement of copies after real medieval
originals, made in south London. The 1856 guide is explicit about
the borrowing: this bay's cloister is "of the Decorated period,
founded in its arches and columns on the Abbey of Guisborough,
Yorkshire", and the court around it assembles portions from
Lincoln, Tewkesbury, Wells, Rochester, Southwell, Ely, Beverley
and Lichfield. The model follows the court's own evidenced forms —
the guide's drawing of the court's own arcade — not a generic
Gothic arch, and it must not be presented in the film as a medieval
building or as Guisborough Abbey itself.

## What it is

One repeatable bay of that cloister arcade, spanning
**pier-centre to pier-centre**: two tall pointed **lights**, each
with two moulded orders, springing from a slender central
**mullion shaft** and from the inner shafts of two **clustered
piers**; one **containing arch** of three moulded orders over
both lights; a **quatrefoil panel** centred in the spandrel; and,
above a string course, the **frieze band** of square quatrefoil
panels (one per light) under a stepped cornice — exactly the
stack the guide's "Arcade from Guisborough" woodcut draws.

The end piers are **half clusters**, cut at the module edges
(x = ±1.20 m): copies placed side by side at the 2.40 m pitch
compose full clustered piers, and the arch bands, wall and bands
continue without overlap (verified in the tiled render — see QC).
A single module also stands alone as a clean cut fragment.

Each pier cluster = a main demi-shaft (which carries the
containing arch), an inner side shaft (which carries the light
arch) and a front demi-shaft, on a shared half-octagon plinth and
under a shared abacus slab. All shafts have moulded bases (double
torus rings on the plinth), and **foliate bell capitals**: a
flaring bell with eight lobes standing in for the carved stiff-
leaf foliage of the sources, a necking ring and a moulded abacus.
Shafts are **dark polished stone** under **pale capitals** — the
tonal contrast read directly off the vestibule photograph (dark
Purbeck-like shafts, pale carved capitals), not an invention.

Origin at the base centre of the bay's footprint, ground z = 0.
Units: metres. Blender 4.5.3.

measured on the guide's drawings (visual grounding); absolute

**The absolute-size chain:** (1) in the vestibule photograph the
great arch spans ≈ 255 image units against ≈ 156 units for a bust
on its pedestal (≈ 1.65 m combined) at a comparable depth — an
arch span of ≈ 2.7 m for the vestibule's larger, nave-scale
arches; (2) the cloister bay is a finer, human-scale screen than
the vestibule arch (the p.92 elevation fits four-light groups and
an upper band into the court's aisle height); (3) a 2.40 m bay
with a 4.32 m overall height keeps the woodcut's measured ratios
(light/bay = 0.37, band/arcade ≈ 0.13) at a scale consistent with
(1). Every link is an estimate; **±20% on absolute size would not
surprise**. The *proportions* rest on the measured ratios.

## Paint and polychromy

The guide attests colour in this court — the Rochester doorway
was "coloured", and colouring and gilding were carried "to an
almost extravagant extent in the Gothic style" (p.89) — but **no
source in the collection gives the cloister arcade's own colour
scheme**. The prop therefore ships in the two materials the
photograph attests tonally (dark polished shafts, pale stone
elsewhere) with no invented polychromy, labelled interpretive.
If a colour source for the arcade reaches the repo, repainting is
a material change only.

## Scope — the Renaissance half of item 13

SCENE-BUILD-PLAN item 13 is a *pair*: this Gothic fragment and a
Renaissance fragment, "designed to be shot separately". This
package builds the **Gothic fragment only**, as briefed. The
Renaissance (Italian Court) fragment is unbuilt; evidence for it
exists in the collection (the Italian Court stereograph, round 1)
and it remains a separate build. The two fragments assert no
adjacency and no hierarchy of civilisations, per the plan.

## What was NOT modelled / known inaccuracies

- **The statues.** The p.92 elevation sets statue niches at the
  piers and the p.86 entrance sets figures between its arches.
  Figurative sculpture is never modelled procedurally in this
  project; the niches/figures are omitted, not faked.
- **The sculptured tympanum band and pierced parapet** above the
  frieze in the p.92 elevation (the Lichfield sculpture band):
  relief figure-work, omitted; the prop stops at a plain stepped
  cornice above the quatrefoil frieze — the highest *architectural*
  (non-figural) band the woodcut evidence supports.
- **Capital carving is a lobed bell, not foliage.** Eight radial
  lobes stand in for the photograph's dense stiff-leaf carving;
  in close-up it will not pass as carving. It reads correctly at
  CP040's intended distance.
- **The quatrefoils** are shallow relief (four lobes + centre
  disc in a frame), simplified from the woodcuts' pierced panels.
- **The back** is the plain reverse of the same mouldings (the
  arch bands run full-depth); the sources do not show the
  cloister's back face, and no rear detail is invented.
- **Depth/thickness** of wall and orders is inferred (no section
  source); the orders' *stepping* follows the elevations.
- No weathering, damage or later state: the court as opened in
  1854.

## QC (2026-10-26)

- Sources read at full resolution; the two measured ratios taken
  by visual grounding on enlarged crops (values in the table).
- Five build/render rounds, every render viewed against the
  woodcuts. Defects found and fixed:
  (1) the arch orders were first built with a constant half-span
  instead of constant centres, so the "concentric" orders crossed
  each other and interpenetrated near the springings (black
  patches) — rebuilt with shared centres, differing radii only;
  (2) the two light arches' dropped ends met at the mullion in
  coincident faces (a black diamond) — outlines now clipped at
  the mullion face and inside the pier shaft;
  (3) the containing arch's orders overhung the module edge at
  the springings — outlines clipped at x = ±1.20 m into clean
  vertical cut faces, which is also what makes tiling exact;
  (4) the spandrel wall's outline dropped to the springing line,
  retracing the arch bands' end faces (coincident opposite
  faces, a black band in side view) — the wall now starts at the
  extrados curve;
  (5) half (demi) lathe parts wound inward or open at the cut —
  cut-plane cap strips added and every mesh's orientation
  verified (signed-volume and radial tests); bmesh
  `recalc_face_normals` was abandoned for these hand-built
  meshes after it left individual faces flipped — orientation is
  now set deterministically and verified in the saved file.
- Two alarming black areas in early side views were chased with
  shadow-off and emission-override test renders and proved to be
  **cast shadows** (the cornice stack shadowing the cut faces,
  the front capital shadowing the main capital's cut face), not
  geometry: the emission render shows a clean solid silhouette.
  The fill light was re-aimed at the model so the cut faces read;
  small contact shadows at the cut plane remain, as they would
  on a real cut fragment.
- Tiling verified: three modules side by side compose full
  clustered piers with continuous arches and bands
  (`renders/tiled-three-quarter.png`).
- Counts, verified two ways (re-opening the saved `.blend` and
  parsing the exported `.glb` — both agree): **51 objects,
  13,592 triangles** — well under the ~50,000 triangle prop
  budget. World bounding box: 2.40 m wide × 0.74 m deep
  (pier plinths) × 4.32 m high. All meshes have positive signed
  volume; no flipped faces remain on the cut planes.
- Process note: the final build's render pass was interrupted
  after the front view (the session's process was reaped); the
  side, three-quarter and tiled views were rendered from the
  saved `.blend` with its own cameras and lighting, unchanged —
  all four shipped renders are of the shipped geometry.
- The `.blend` contains two collections: `Model` (exported) and
  `RenderSetup` (ground plane, lights, cameras; not exported).
  Meshes are world-space geometry with unit scale throughout.
