# References — sy36-ruin-fragment-bay

All sources are from this repo's own collections (round 1
`references/muse-drive/`, round 2 `references/muse-round2/` on
branch `muse-round2`). No outside images were used.

## The 1936 state (what is modelled)

| File (copy in this folder) | Repo source | Licence | What it establishes |

| `fire-1936-aerial-morning-after.jpg` | `references/muse-drive/images/main-article/Crystal_Palace_Destroyed_1936.jpg` (review gallery 0587/0588) | Public domain | Aerial view of the site the morning after the fire of 30 Nov 1936. The whole building is down: a low field of tangled wreckage inside the perimeter, essentially nothing standing above the ground-floor walls except the water tower. ## Failure behaviour at ground level (how the members failed)

| File | Repo source | Licence | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ruins-1867-iln-ground-level.jpg` | `references/muse-round2/images/S67-01_iln-1867-01-12_ruins-abu-simbel_north-transept.jpg` — ILN, 12 Jan 1867 | Public domain | Ground-level view of **the same structure** (Sydenham palace) after the fire of 30 Dec 1866. The repo's 1936 photographs are aerial/distant, so this engraving is the collection's readable ground-level record of how this building's ironwork fails in fire: slender members bent into **smooth heat arcs** (one tall member bows over in a single curve at the right), thin rods hanging/drooping in wilted curves, some stripped uprights and a truss fragment still standing at the left, and a foreground mat of flat, interlaced straight lengths. The model's bends, droops and the standing kinked stump follow these behaviours. It is used for failure *behaviour* only — the 1866 fire destroyed one end of the palace; the 1936 fire, which the prop depicts, destroyed all of it. |

## The members themselves (what was wrecked)

Modelled dimensions come from the Downes 1852 plates and the repo's
control table, not from the photographs:

- `references/downes-1852/crystal-palace-1851/CONTROL-DIMENSIONS.md`
  — D06 (24 ft bay), D09 (8 ft gutter pitch), D10 (3 ft girder
  depth), D13/D14 (column: 8 in bore; tier-1 column 18 ft 11¾ in),
  D16 (base plates 2 ft × 1 ft).
- `models/hyde-park-column-tier1/NOTES.md` — column as built from
  Plate VIII: 10 in outer diameter over the 8 in bore, 6 in floor
  collar, girder section. The wrecked columns and girder here use
  the same figures.
- `models/hp-roof-furrow-bay/NOTES.md` — the ridge-and-furrow unit
  from Plate XII: 8 ft furrow pitch, slopes at 2½:1, sash bars
  1 in × 1⅜ in at 10½ in centres, strong bars 2½ in (three per
  24 ft), panes 49 in long, ridge piece 3 in wide, Paxton timber
  gutter (5 in base, 6 in deep overall, 2½ in channel).
- Floor boards 1½ in thick, ½ in gaps, 9 in board width class —
  1851 *Official Catalogue* introduction pp. 62–63 (round 2,
  S25-01/S25-02).

## Not used

- "Melted glass from the Crystal Palace" (London Fire Brigade
  Museum, CC BY 2.0) is catalogued in the round-1 manifest but was
  **skipped-budget and never downloaded**, so it is not in the repo
  and was not used. The model's few glass shards are generic pans
  of the documented 49 in sheet size, not the fused museum lump.
- Historic England Archive OP04456 (twisted metal after the 1866
  fire) is URL-only in the round-2 manifest (no open licence) and
  was not used.
