# References — hp-working-machine-exhibit (Nasmyth steam hammer, 1851)

What each source establishes, and what it does NOT. Full reasoning in
`../SPIKE.md`. Hyde Park 1851 only; no Sydenham blending.

## Identity and form — the exhibit itself

- **Official Descriptive and Illustrated Catalogue (1851), Class VI,
  No. 236: "NASMYTH, J., Manchester. Steam hammer."**
  https://www.gracesguide.co.uk/index.php?title=1851_Great_Exhibition:_Official_Catalogue:_Class_VI.:_J._Nasmyth&oldid=60616
  Establishes: the exhibit, its exhibitor and class. Gives NO
  dimension, weight or fall — checked; this was the decisive open
  source for the exhibit's own dimension, and it yields none.
  Its bracketed editorial note claims only the *capability* of
  graduating the blow to egg-shell force — not a performed stunt.
- **Ferrier photograph, RCIN 2800024** (Reports by the Juries,
  Vol. II), Royal Collection Trust:
  https://www.rct.uk/collection/2800024/the-great-exhibition-1851-steam-hammer-by-nasmyth
  Establishes: the exhibit's FORM — two A-form standards, cylinder
  on top, bolted valve chest, hand lever left, valve rod, low
  handwheel, overhead gear, tup at rest just above the anvil — and
  all relative levels/widths in SPIKE.md §6 (photo-measured).
  Carries NO scale (no figure in frame): form/identity only.
  Local copy: `ferrier-1851-steam-hammer-rcin2800024.jpg`
  (SSPL copy via the Science and Industry Museum blog);
  tup/anvil detail crop: `ferrier-crop-tup-anvil.png`.
- **British Library records C.44.h.5, 2(426b) and X.1067, 2(426b)**
  (same Ferrier print; jury-report quotation "(236)").
- **Council Medal** for the steam hammer: Popular Science Monthly,
  Vol. 51 (May 1897), "Sketch of James Nasmyth".

## Mechanism only — NOT dimension sources

- **Science Museum Group, co46218** (1:4 model of the FIRST hammer,
  1839 design / 1842 patent, Wilson's 1843 gear):
  https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co46218/model-of-james-nasmyths-direct-action-steam-hammer-models-representations-steam-hammers
  Establishes ONLY the pattern's mechanism: single-acting
  direct-action, tup + piston rod guided between two standards,
  steam below the piston lifts, gravity drops, slide valve in a
  chest worked by hand lever, Wilson's self-acting regulating
  device. Per the 2026-10-27 spike guardrail correction it is NOT
  a model of the 1851 exhibit and NO scale is taken from it.
  Local copy: `smg-model-co46218.jpg` (model photograph, for
  mechanism/form comparison only).
- **IMechE 1:8 model**: based on the Patricroft works machine with
  the 1847 self-acting improvement — a later form, not the exhibit;
  not used.

## Explicitly NOT used (other hammers)

- Nasmyth, *Autobiography* (ed. Smiles, 1883): the **30 cwt / 4 ft
  fall Patricroft works demonstration hammer** (after the June 1842
  patent); the **next order, Rushton & Eckersley of Bolton,
  5 tons / 5 ft fall**; the **4-ton pile driver**; the **50-cwt
  Admiralty order**; the Devonport Dockyard **2½-ton** egg
  demonstration (Ch. 15). None of these is the 1851 exhibit.
- **SMG co46229** (surviving full-size hammer, Patricroft 1845–55,
  Royal Mint until 1933): a different, "small version" machine;
  its SMG text is cited only for the priority wording (Nasmyth
  "did not invent the steam hammer, nor was he the first to
  build one"):
  https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co46229/nasmyths-steam-hammer

## Egg stunt — unresolved secondary-source conflict

- The Autobiography attaches the egg passages to the Patricroft
  hammer and to the Devonport Dockyard demonstration; Science
  Museum blog "If I Had A Hammer"
  (https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/if-i-had-a-hammer/) relocates
  an egg demo to the 1851 Exhibition. Conflict unresolved: CP024
  shows generic tup motion only; no egg is staged or claimed.

## Priority — the narrow claim only

Steam-hammer priority is contested (Bourdon/Schneider, Le Creusot
1840). This package claims ONLY: Nasmyth & Co exhibited a steam
hammer at the Great Exhibition in 1851 (Catalogue Class VI,
No. 236; Council Medal). Never "inventor of the steam hammer".
