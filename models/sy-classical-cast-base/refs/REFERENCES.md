# References — sy-classical-cast-base

All sources are already in the repo's round-1 collection
(`references/muse-drive/`); licences are the round-1 manifest's.
No source from outside the repo was used. Sydenham (1854) evidence
only — the one post-1854 item (Cain stereo) is listed as a rejected
variant, not as evidence for the model.

## Primary evidence (the model is built from these)

1. **Delamotte album page, "Greek and Roman Sculpture Court"**
   (lower-left photograph of the four-view album page).
   Repo: `references/muse-drive/images/commons/-Assyrian_Court,_Facade_Towards_the_Nave;_Elevated_View_of_Assyrian_Court;_Greek_and_Roman_Sculpture_Court;_The_Three_Graces-_MET_DP323102.jpg`
   Licence: CC0 (Metropolitan Museum of Art, DP323102).
   Copy here: `delamotte-album-courts.jpg`; enlarged crop of the
   court view: `crop-delamotte-court-elevated.png`.
   Philip Henry Delamotte's photographs of the palace under
   construction/opening (1854–55). Elevated view of the whole
   sculpture court: dozens of casts on the same dark square
   pedestal family — tall ones under standing figures, taller
   narrow ones under busts, round drums under urns, a low wide
   platform under the Farnese Bull group.

2. **Stereo view "101. Statues in the Greek and Roman sculpture
   court"** (Rijksmuseum RP-F-2012-96-327).
   Repo: `references/muse-drive/images/commons/Beelden_in_de_Griekse_en_Romeinse_zaal_in_Crystal_Palace_in_Sydenham_Crystal_Palace._101._Statues_in_the_Greek_and_Roman_sculpture_court._(titel_op_object),_RP-F-2012-96-327.jpg`
   Licence: CC0. Copy here: `stereo-greek-roman-sculpture-court.jpg`;
   left-eye crop: `crop-stereo-court-eye-level.png`.
   Dated c.1870–c.1890 by the Rijksmuseum, but it shows the same
   court and the same dark pedestal family as Delamotte's 1850s
   view — the type persisted. This is the profile source: the
   eye-level view of the "Aristides or Aeschines" pedestal
   (`crop-pedestal-aristides.png`) and the "Minerva" bust pedestal
   (`crop-pedestal-minerva.png`) show the plain die, the
   plinth-step + cove base moulding, the absence of a cap
   moulding, and the painted number-and-title label on the die.
   All dimensions were measured on these two crops (see NOTES.md).

## Corroborating / context

3. **1856 guide, p.55, "The Greek Court"**.
   Repo: `references/muse-drive/images/commons/Guide_to_the_Crystal_Palace_and_Park_1856_(147900427).jpg`
   Licence: public domain. Copy here: `guide-1856-p55-greek-court.jpg`.
   Attests the court's contents (Laocoon, Farnese Juno, Discobolus,
   Barberini Faun, Venus of Milo "in the centre") and "the busts of
   the Greek Poets… a portion of the Portrait Gallery" — the bust
   displays the tall pedestal variant served. The court's
   architectural colouring is described as "blue, red, and yellow
   surfaces, blazoned with gold" — that is the court architecture,
   not these pedestals, which the photographs show plain and dark.
   The guide gives no pedestal description or dimension.

4. **Delamotte album page, "The Three Graces"** (lower-right
   photograph of the same album page as item 1). Crop here:
   `crop-three-graces-base.png`. The Pilon monument's own carved
   Renaissance base stands on a low, plain, dark rectangular block
   — a further member of the same dark-plinth family (variant,
   documented in NOTES.md, not built).

5. **Delamotte, "Andromeda, John Bell"** (MET DP323119, cropped).
   Repo: `references/muse-drive/images/commons/_ANDROMEDA___JOHN_BELL__from_-Sculptures_of_Andromeda,_the_Toilet_of_Atalanta,_Corinna,_and_a_Naiad-_MET_DP323119_(cropped).jpg`
   Licence: CC0. Copy here: `andromeda-john-bell-met.jpg`.
   A modern (1851-exhibition) statue among the nave planting on a
   low dark pedestal with painted label "7 / ANDROMEDA / JOHN
   BELL" — the low nave variant (documented, not built).

## Rejected as evidence for this prop (different date/context)

6. **Stereo view "239. Cain. By A. Etex", Sculpture Gallery**
   (Rijksmuseum RP-F-2012-96-336, CC0; c.1870–c.1890).
   Copy here: `stereo-cain-sculpture-gallery.jpg`.
   Shows the later Sculpture Gallery's light-coloured, panelled,
   fully moulded pedestal — a different, later furniture type.
   Recorded as a variant in NOTES.md; NOT used, because this prop
   is the 1854 classical-court standard.
