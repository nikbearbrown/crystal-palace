# REFERENCES — sy-concert-organ-front

The pipe front of the great concert organ of the Sydenham Crystal
Palace central transept — Gray & Davison, built expressly for the
first Handel Festival (15–19 June 1857) and in use for the
triennial festivals until 1926. **Sydenham only.**

## Dimensioned contemporary source (web, public domain)

- *The Engineer*, 2 June 1882, "The Great Organ at the Crystal
  Palace" (pp. 393–394, 400): `engineer-1882-06-02-great-organ.pdf`
  (full issue excerpt, via Graces Guide) and the p.400 plate
  extracted as `engineer-1882-section-p400.jpg` — "SECTION THROUGH
  THE GREAT ORGAN AT THE CRYSTAL PALACE. Messrs. Gray and Davison,
  Builders, Euston Road, London."
  - "The organ stands on a stout platform or floor, **40 ft wide
    by about 25 ft deep**…"
  - "The space occupied by the instrument is **40 ft wide by
    24 ft deep**, exclusive of that taken up by the five bellows…"
  - "the largest being a wooden pipe **2 ft square and 32 ft
    long**" (the pedal CCCC — internal; the section plate
    annotates the pedal ranks "32 FEET"). Not a front pipe.
  - Layout described and drawn: blowing apparatus below (A),
    console in front (C), great organ (D) at the front, solo (E)
    above, swell (F) at the very top, pedal soundboards (GG) on
    each side. As enlarged in 1882: 82 stops, 4,394 pipes.
  - The article describes the 1882 **reconstruction/enlargement**;
    the case front it stood behind is the one photographed in 0057
    (before) and 0595 (after), unchanged in arrangement.

## Corroboration of the festival configuration

- Grove, *A Dictionary of Music and Musicians*, "Handel Festival"
  (Wikisource): preliminary festival held 15, 17, 19 June 1857 in
  the Central Transept; "a grand organ, built by Gray and Davison
  expressly for the occasion"; choir upwards of 2,000, orchestra
  396, conductor Michael Costa.
- Wikipedia, "Handel Festival 1857" — same facts, citing Grove.

## Not used

- The 1851 Hyde Park organs (Gray & Davison's and Willis's
  exhibition instruments) — different building, different organ.
- Any generic Victorian organ case: the front follows the two
  Sydenham photographs, not a pattern book.
