# References — hp-great-elm

All evidence is already inside the project repo `nikbearbrown/crystal-palace`.
Local copies of the three views sit in this folder. They are **Hyde Park
1851** views — no Sydenham evidence is used for this prop.

## Copied into this folder

### 1. `Great_Exhibition,_Transept_looking_south,_HF_Talbot,_1851.jpg`
- **Repo path:** `references/muse-drive/images/commons/Great_Exhibition,_Transept_looking_south,_HF_Talbot,_1851.jpg`
- **What it is:** Henry Fox Talbot calotype photograph, 1851, captioned
  "View of transept, looking south". The great elm stands at the far
  (south) end of the transept, silhouetted against the great end window,
  with the Crystal Fountain in the middle distance.
- **What was taken from it:** the elm's proportions and silhouette —
  the primary source for this prop. Measured on the image (normalised
  plate coordinates, visual grounding): end-window arch crown y=135,
  elm canopy top y=218, fork of the elm y=315, floor at the tree y=424,
  canopy left/right extent x=248/597. Against the transept vault crown
  height of 102 ft 2 in (31.16 m, 1856 guide table for Hyde Park) this
  gives an elm height of ≈ 22.2 m and a fork at ≈ 11.8 m. The crown is
  a broad fan: much wider than it is deep, widest in its top third,
  with long limbs radiating upward and outward and foliage layered
  along them; the bole is straight and clear well above the gallery.
- **Licence:** Public domain (round-1 manifest).

### 2. `The_Transept_(BM_1917,1208.2277_1).jpg`
- **Repo path:** `references/muse-drive/images/commons/The_Transept_(BM_1917,1208.2277_1).jpg`
- **What it is:** Dickinson Brothers lithograph after Joseph Nash,
  "The Great Industrial Exhibition of 1851", Plate 4, *The Transept* —
  looking along the transept toward the Crystal Fountain, the elms
  rising behind and around it (British Museum 1917,1208.2277).
- **What was taken from it:** corroboration of the canopy character in
  full summer leaf — a dense, billowing mass filling the upper
  transept, crown tops well below the vault crown, foliage beginning
  around gallery height, and the trees reading as a group behind the
  fountain (scene CP022). As a lithograph it is used for character,
  not measurement.
- **Licence:** Public domain (round-1 manifest).

### 3. `Crystal_Palace_-_interior.jpg`
- **Repo path:** `references/muse-drive/images/main-article/Crystal_Palace_-_interior.jpg`
- **What it is:** McNeven coloured lithograph, 1851, "The Foreign
  Department, viewed towards the transept" (Ackermann).
- **What was taken from it:** transept context only — the view toward
  the crossing from the nave. The elms are not individually readable
  in it; it was **not** used for the tree's shape or size.
- **Licence:** Public domain (round-1 manifest).

## Not used

- No photograph or plate isolates a single transept elm closely enough
  to model an individual tree — hence this prop is a **generic**
  procedural elm (see NOTES.md). Modern photographs of elms were not
  used: no photo textures or outside-repo material of any kind.
