# committee-brick-model — build notes

**What this is:** a scale model *of* the 1850 Building Committee's
official brick design for the Great Exhibition building — the heavy
brick scheme, with Brunel's great sheet-iron dome, that Paxton's glass
design displaced — presented the way the film's design-race scene
(CP007) needs it: as a period architect's tabletop model on a base
board. The board, edge trim and miniature scale are the point of the
prop; it is deliberately *not* dressed as a full-size building.

**Presentation scale: 1:500.** The model building is 1.2192 m long on a
1.38 × 0.42 m board, 0.130 m high overall. No period model of this
scheme is recorded — the tabletop presentation is the film's staging
device (per the build plan: "small volumetric model of the committee
scheme on a worktable"), not a claim that this object existed in 1850.

Units are metres; origin at the base centre of the board (the board's
bottom face); object scale applied.

## The scheme — sources

The design was published in the press in June 1850 and was widely
ridiculed. Form and dimensions rest on three period plates (copies in
`refs/`, full citations in `refs/REFERENCES.md`):

1. **ILN, 22 June 1850, p. 445** — perspective (from the south-west)
   over the ground plan, the plan carrying a printed scale bar of
   0–1000 ft. The controlling source.
2. **Berlyn & Fowler, *The Crystal Palace* (1851), p. 22** — "Ground
   Plan of the Building Committee's Design", with key (9 = The
   Rotunda). Plan check.
3. **Berlyn & Fowler (1851), p. 24** — "Exterior View of the Building
   Committee's Design", perspective from the south-east. Height
   ratios. (Catalogued in the round-1 manifest but skipped there;
   fetched from its recorded Commons URL — see REFERENCES.md.)

Published descriptions: the building was a brick design in the
Rundbogenstil by **Thomas Leverton Donaldson**, with a **sheet-iron
dome by I. K. Brunel, published as 200 ft in diameter** (Victorian Web,
quoting Dugan; Wikipedia concurs on attribution). Contemporary
ridicule held it would need **15 million bricks** and 18 months to
build, against Paxton's months — the practical case it lost on.

## The scheme — its real intended size (as published / measured)

| Element | Adopted | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Overall length | **2,000 ft (609.6 m)** | measured on the ILN plan against its printed scale bar: the bar's 0–1000 ft spans ≈1,350–1,375 px of the scan and the drawn building ≈2,767 px → 2,010–2,050 ft by two readings; adopted 2,000 ft nominal (±3 %) |
| Depth | **≈470 ft (143 m)** | ratio from the Berlyn & Fowler plan (plate is vertically squashed ≈1.35×; corrected depth/length ratio 0.234 × 2,000 ft); ±10 % |
| Rotunda position | centre at **46.5 % of the length from the west end** | both plans agree (46.0–46.9 % measured) |
| Dome diameter | **200 ft (61.0 m)** | **published figure** (see above). The rotunda ring as drawn measures ≈220 ft overall — the dome is modelled at the published 200 ft |
| Dome springing / total height | ≈95 ft / **≈175 ft (53 m)** | ratio from the p.24 perspective (total height ≈0.88 × dome Ø), ±15 % — engraver's perspective, not a measured elevation |
| Main ranges | eaves ≈48 ft, ridge ≈82 ft | p.24 perspective ratios (≈0.24 / ≈0.40 × dome Ø), ±15 % |
| Great cross-gables | at ≈29 % and ≈73 % of the length; apex ≈88 ft; great arch ≈52 ft wide, top ≈66 ft | positions from the plan axes (refreshment-court cross axes); heights from the perspectives, ±15 % |
| End pavilions | ≈140 ft long, ridge ≈55 ft | perspectives |

Where the two perspectives disagree in detail (they do — feature
spacing along the front differs between them), the **plan governs
positions** and the p.24 view governs height ratios; both are stated
as readings, not facts. There is no surviving measured elevation of
the scheme in the collection.

## The model at 1:500

Long brick ranges under a continuous pitched slate roof; ground floor
a round-headed arcade (the Rundbogenstil character) with a pent-roof
colonnade on posts along both long fronts; two great cross-gable
fronts (one toward each end) each with a tall five-light arched
window, flanking oculi and its own cross roof; three smaller gabled
porches per front (the centre one, on the rotunda axis, larger);
stepped-down end pavilions with wide end arches. At the centre the
circular Great Hall (rotunda) carries the drum — clerestory band of
28 small rectangular windows between stone cornices — and the ribbed
sheet-iron dome (24 ribs, texture-drawn), elliptical in profile and
slightly pointed at the crown as the engravings show, with a small cap
and finial.

Materials are procedural throughout (generated by
`scripts/make_textures.py`, nothing photographed): running-bond brick
drawn at true model scale — a course is 0.15 mm at 1:500, so it reads
as fine masonry grain, kept deliberately subtle; stone arcade, drum
and dome-skin textures drawn the same way; board in grained wood with
a darker edge trim; a blank brass plaque on the front edge (a
presentational device of the prop, no inscription invented).

## What was NOT modelled

- Window openings in the upper walls (the engravings show the ranges
  almost blank above the arcade; at 1:500 any indication would be
  texture noise).
- The interior: the rotunda, refreshment courts and hall divisions
  are plan information only; the model is a solid massing miniature.
- Individual dome ribs as geometry (texture ribs instead — at
  122 mm dome diameter the relief would be sub-millimetre).
- Site context: no ground, planting, people, or the Hyde Park setting
  — the board is the whole world of this prop.
- Any inscription on the plaque (none is evidenced; left blank).

## Honest uncertainties

- The total length rests on one engraver's scale bar; the two printed
  plans agree with each other, and 2,000 ft is consistent with the
  site the Palace later occupied, but no published overall dimension
  for the scheme was found.
- All heights are perspective ratios (±15 %); the 1856-style
  "measured elevation" for this scheme does not exist in the sources.
- The published 200 ft dome diameter is a secondary-source figure
  (Dugan via Victorian Web); it is adopted over the ≈220 ft the plan
  drawing measures for the rotunda ring, and the difference is
  recorded here rather than smoothed over.
- French 1849 and New York exhibition plans were **not** used — they
  are not London geometry (build-plan rule for this item). Richard
  Turner's rival design was likewise identified in search results and
  kept out: it is a different scheme.

## QC log

- 2026-10-27, build r1: geometry built clean; 8,096 triangles
  (evaluated count from the build script). First renders showed four
  defects, all fixed in r2: (1) arch-panel outlines were wound the
  wrong way over the crown, folding the n-gon faces into black
  wedges; (2) flat material colours had been set as linear values
  and rendered ~2× too bright (slate roofs near-white) — now
  converted from sRGB, and the brick/arcade/glazing textures deepened;
  (3) prism normals made deterministic with a signed-volume winding
  check; (4) oculi moved down the gable wall, clear of the cross-roof.
- 2026-10-27, build r2 QC pass (12-sample renders, all views
  inspected): a bounding-box audit then found the real cause of the
  "dark ramps" flanking the drum — the cross-gable and porch roof
  prisms were built centred on x = 0 and never offset to their
  gable/porch positions (the gable blocks stood roofless, the missing
  roofs were stacked at the rotunda). `prism()` gained a centre
  parameter; roofs now sit on their blocks in all three views.
- 2026-10-27, build r3 (final): full 64-sample renders inspected —
  front, side and three-quarter all match the engravings' composition
  (arcaded ranges, pent-roof colonnades, two glazed cross-gables with
  oculi, porches, stepped end pavilions, ribbed dome on clerestory
  drum, board + trim + plaque). GLB verified independently in a fresh
  Blender process: 101 objects, **8,096 triangles**, bounds
  1.3800 × 0.4213 × 0.1383 m, base at z = 0 (origin at the board's
  base centre) as specified. Under the 50k budget.
