# References — committee-brick-model

The 1850 Building Committee's official brick design for the Great
Exhibition building (the scheme Paxton's glass design displaced).
All period sources are public domain. Copies were taken from the
project's own round-1 collection (`references/muse-drive/`) unless
noted.

## Primary sources used

1. **ILN, 22 June 1850, p. 445** — "Building for the Great Industrial
   Exhibition, to be erected in Hyde-Park": perspective view from the
   south-west printed over the ground plan, the plan carrying a printed
   scale bar (0–1000 ft).
   - Repo: `references/muse-drive/images/iln-1850/iln-1850_04.jpg`
   - Copy: `iln-1850-06-22-p445-committee-design.jpg`
   - Licence: public domain (Illustrated London News, 1850).
   - **The controlling source.** All plan dimensions were measured from
     this sheet against its own scale bar; the form reading (arcaded
     brick ranges, pent-roof corridors, ribbed dome on a clerestory
     drum, great arched cross-gables) comes from its perspective.

2. **Berlyn & Fowler, *The Crystal Palace: its architectural history
   and conducting principles* (1851), p. 22** — "Ground Plan of the
   Building Committee's Design for the Great Exhibition of 1851", with
   key (9 = The Rotunda, 4 = Refreshment Courts, etc.).
   - Repo: `references/muse-drive/images/commons/The_Crystal_Palace_page_22.jpg`
   - Copy: `berlyn-fowler-1851-p22-committee-ground-plan.jpg`
   - Licence: public domain.
   - Used for the plan check: rotunda centre at ≈46–47 % of the length
     from the west end; block depth ≈0.234 × length (the plate is
     vertically squashed ≈1.35×, so only horizontal measurements and
     the corrected depth ratio were taken from it).

3. **Berlyn & Fowler (1851), p. 24** — "Exterior View of the Building
   Committee's Design": perspective from the south-east.
   - Copy: `berlyn-fowler-1851-p24-committee-exterior.jpg`
   - Catalogue status: listed in the round-1 manifest
     (`references/muse-drive/data/manifest.csv`, section
     `commons-book`, row for `The_Crystal_Palace_page_24.jpg`) but
     marked `skipped-budget` — no local file existed in the collection.
     Fetched 2026-10-27 from the manifest's own recorded `original_url`
     (Wikimedia Commons, public domain; 2747 × 1625, 616,700 bytes).
   - Used for the height ratios (total height ≈0.88 × dome diameter;
     main ridge ≈0.40 × dome diameter) and to confirm the two great
     arched gable fronts, one toward each end of the building.

## Published descriptions (web, checked 2026-10-27)

- *The Victorian Web*, "The Building Committee's Design and Paxton's
  Response" (victorianweb.org, quoting John Dugan): the committee's
  design "featured **a sheet iron dome two hundred feet in diameter**",
  the building "squatly Romanesque with a whiff of Byzantine".
  → the adopted dome diameter: **200 ft**.
- Wikipedia, "The Crystal Palace": the standby design was "a brick
  building in the Rundbogenstil style by Donaldson with a sheet-iron
  dome by Brunel", rejected as impractical when published.
  → attribution: Thomas Leverton Donaldson (architecture),
  I. K. Brunel (dome).
- Contemporary ridicule repeated in secondary sources: the scheme
  **would have required some 15 million bricks** and an 18-month lead
  time — the practical case Paxton's design answered. (Context only;
  not used for geometry.)

## Explicitly NOT used

- French (Paris 1849) and New York exhibition plans — foreign schemes,
  excluded by the build plan.
- Richard Turner's rival iron-and-glass design (200 ft centre span,
  408 ft width, 100 ft high, per an ICE proceedings paper surfaced in
  web search) — a *different* design-race entry, not the committee's;
  none of its figures were used.
- The Crystal Palace as built (1,848 ft × 408 ft) — Paxton's building,
  not this scheme; not used for any dimension, though the similar
  order of length is a useful sanity check on the plan measurement.
