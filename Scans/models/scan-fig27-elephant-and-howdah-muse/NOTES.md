# NOTES — scan-fig27-elephant-and-howdah (Muse version)

**Source:** fig-27_john-nash-indian-elephant-howdah.png — John Nash's
view of the India Court exhibit: a stuffed Indian elephant and
howdah from India (catalog fig. 27, IMG_6460, p.103).

## Subject
The exhibit as arranged in the image:
- A life-size **Indian** elephant (simplified, clean low-poly form),
  standing on a two-step plinth, wearing a gold caparison with a deep
  red-brown emblem panel and fringe, and white garland strands at the
  neck. Smaller ears and the twin-domed forehead follow the Indian
  species as shown; long tusks and curled trunk as in the image.
- The **howdah on its back**: white railed platform and a tall gold
  domed canopy on four poles, with valance fringe — read from the
  tall canopy aligned with the elephant in the image.
- A **second canopied howdah displayed on a white stand** to the
  right rear, as in the image.
- A **draped textile backdrop** behind the elephant (swags and
  hanging drapes in muted red/ochre/slate) representing the court's
  hanging textiles, deliberately abstract.
- A **rope barrier** on posts in front of the plinth, as displayed.
- Three **attendant figures** (two adults, one child) in stylised
  Indian dress beside the elephant's near flank, as composed in the
  image — mannequin-like, **no facial features, no likenesses**.

## Scale
Elephant ≈ 2.8 m at the shoulder (brief range 2.7–3 m for an Indian
elephant), body ≈ 3.7 m long excluding trunk/tusks; howdah canopy
crown ≈ 5.3 m above the floor. Plinth, stand and figures sized from
the image's internal proportions. **Estimated** — no dimensioned
source; anchored on the brief's shoulder-height range.

## Assumptions / interpretation
- Single three-quarter view: the elephant's far side, the caparison's
  rear, and the howdah's construction are interpretation. The
  caparison side panels are flat hanging panels; the woven pattern
  and the emblem's motif (illegible in the image) are represented by
  a plain deep panel, not invented ornament.
- Arrangement call: the tall gold canopy is modelled as the
  elephant's own howdah (its poles land on the back platform, as the
  image suggests); the smaller canopy to the right is a separate
  displayed howdah. Both readings are visible in the image; the
  alternative (both canopies on stands behind a bare-backed elephant)
  is noted here as a **manual-review item**.
- The elephant is a taxidermy exhibit in the source; the model is a
  clean stylised animal form, not a sculptural study — surface
  detail (skin folds, toenails) is intentionally not modelled.
- The Crystal Palace architecture, gallery visitors and neighbouring
  exhibits visible in the image are NOT modelled (not integral to
  this subject); the backdrop textiles are reduced to one draped
  wall section.
- Colours follow the colour illustration (gold howdah/caparison,
  grey elephant, red-brown emblem panel).
