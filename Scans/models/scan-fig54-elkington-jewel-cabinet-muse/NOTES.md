# NOTES — scan-fig54-elkington-jewel-cabinet (Muse version)

**Subject.** Jewel cabinet designed by Louis (Ludwig) Gruner for Elkington &
Co., 1851 (Royal Collection), as photographed in fig-54 of the Scans catalog
(front view). This package is the Muse version of the subject, built for the
Crystal Palace film project.

**What was modelled** (front view as the reference):
- Chamfered-corner cabinet carcass in giltwood/bronze tones on a stepped,
  chamfered plinth; plinth frieze with gilt scroll curls and four blue enamel
  portrait medallions (plain bust silhouettes — no facial detail invented).
- Two large portrait panels with pale patterned enamel borders (instanced
  diamond pattern): Queen Victoria with a child (left) and Prince Albert
  (right), rendered as abstract dark silhouettes on dark panels — the
  paintings' likenesses are NOT reproduced.
- Central arched niche with a small silvered female figure on a pedestal
  (stylised, simplified).
- Full-length silvered female figures standing on plinths at both chamfered
  front corners (stylised draped figures, no facial detail).
- Dentilled cornice; stepped gilt roof slopes with a blue fish-scale enamel
  panel on the front slope (procedurally generated scallop texture).
- Crest: two silvered putti flanking a pedestal with a gilt lion finial
  (stylised heraldic lion).

**Scale.** ESTIMATED. Overall width 1.80 m, height to lion ≈ 1.45 m, depth
0.58 m — furniture scale in the 1.5–2 m class, estimated from the image
(the photograph carries no scale reference). All internal proportions follow
the photograph.

**Assumptions and unseen sides.**
- The scan shows the FRONT only. The back and the roof plan are interpreted
  symmetrically (chamfered plan continued, roof stepped on all sides); the
  interior, hinges, locks and any back decoration are not modelled and are
  unknown.
- The portrait panels' painted content is abstracted to silhouettes; the
  real panels are enamel/painted portraits.
- The corner and niche figures are simplified stand-ins for Elkington's
  modelled silver figures; their exact poses follow the photograph only
  approximately.
- REPAIR NOTE: this scan has one repaired area — a tiny upper-left
  BACKGROUND corner (modern infill, 0.2% of pixels, per catalog.json).
  It is background only; no part of the cabinet was reconstructed from
  repaired pixels, and the repair was not used as evidence for anything.

**Counts / QC.** 42,196 triangles (Model collection, evaluated). Objects are
named by part; border pattern and dentils share instanced geometry. Preview
rendered from a near-frontal angle comparable to the reference photograph,
neutral grey backdrop, Cycles CPU.

**Files.** `scan-fig54-elkington-jewel-cabinet.blend`,
`scan-fig54-elkington-jewel-cabinet.glb`, `preview.png`,
`build_scan_fig54_elkington_jewel_cabinet.py` (re-runnable build script).

**Manual review.** Recommended: (1) overall scale is estimated — check
against the Royal Collection's published dimensions if the model is used
at hero distance; (2) portrait panel treatment is deliberately abstract.
