# NOTES — scan-fig73-centenary-model-1951 (Muse version)

**Subject.** The Crystal Palace centenary pavilion at the Festival of
Britain, 1951, as photographed in fig-73 of the Scans catalog (caption:
"Scale model of the Crystal Palace, centenary pavilion, Festival of
Britain 1951"). NOTE on the subject: although the caption calls it a
scale model, the photograph shows a large walk-in replica section of the
palace — a barrel-vaulted nave with a transept front, big enough for
visitors to walk through — displayed on a raised deck. The model follows
the photograph, not the caption's wording. This package is the Muse
version of the subject, built for the Crystal Palace film project.

**What was modelled**
- The replica pavilion: white painted-iron colonnade of round arches on
  both long sides, crimson swag frieze with gilt rosettes below the
  eaves, crenellated eaves trim, ribbed barrel-vault roof with a
  translucent glass shell, and a glazed fan gable at the nave end.
- The transept crossing toward one end: taller cross vault, front gable
  with a radial-spoked fan, three arched openings at deck level, and the
  draped emblem panel — modelled as a crimson roundel with a gilt star
  between two drape swags. The panel's printed numerals "1851 / 1951"
  are lettering and are NOT reproduced.
- The raised display deck: lattice-pattern railings (as photographed),
  rows of slender undercroft columns, and a stair hint in the shadow
  beneath the deck.
- Interior hints seen through the arches: two elm trees and dark display
  blocks.
- Seven visitors on the deck in 1950s silhouettes (suits and flat caps,
  dresses, one child) at the positions suggested by the photograph —
  stylised figures, no likenesses. They also set the scale.

**Scale.** ESTIMATED from the visitors in the photograph (~1.7 m):
pavilion ~13.2 m long, deck ~2.6 m above the ground, vault crown ~7.4 m
overall, transept crown ~7.8 m. The photograph carries no dimension;
treat all sizes as photo-estimated.

**Assumptions and unseen sides.**
- The photograph is a single three-quarter view. The far side and far
  end repeat the near side's arches/frieze; the interior is only hinted
  (the real pavilion interior displays are not modelled in detail).
- The source is monochrome: the white ironwork and glass are read
  directly, but the crimson of the swags/drapes is interpretive (1951
  bunting colours), as are the visitors' clothes colours.
- The undercroft is simplified: the photograph shows a forest of
  supports and a stair in deep shadow; the model gives the column rhythm
  and one stair run.
- The ground the pavilion stood on (festival site surroundings) is not
  modelled beyond the neutral backdrop.

**Counts / QC.** 32,428 triangles (Model collection, evaluated).
Preview rendered from a raised three-quarter angle comparable to the
photograph, neutral grey backdrop, Cycles CPU, 48 samples + denoise.
QC fix during the build: the first framing was too close and cropped
the vault — camera pulled back so the whole pavilion, deck and
undercroft are in frame, verified against the photograph.
