# NOTES — scan-fig53-virginian-slave (Muse version)

**Subject and historical context.** John Tenniel, "Virginian Slave",
*Punch*, 7 June 1851 (fig-53 of the Scans catalog). Published during the
Great Exhibition, the print is a British satire aimed at the United States:
it parodies Hiram Powers' *Greek Slave* (fig-52) by presenting an enslaved
African-American woman, chained, standing on a military-drum pedestal
inscribed "E PLVRIBVS VNVM" (E Pluribus Unum), leaning against a broken
column draped with the American flag — a pointed contrast between
American liberty rhetoric and American slavery.

**What was modelled** (the composition, not the caricature):
- Cylindrical drum pedestal on a wide base ring carrying the motto
  "E PLVRIBVS VNVM" in raised letters around the front (spelling as
  printed); rope moulding at the drum's top rim.
- Drum ornament as drawn: three rings with two sagging chain swags, a
  pair of crossed rifles (simplified to tapered batons) and a tassel.
- Broken column beside the figure with a draped Stars and Stripes
  (procedurally generated flag texture — stripes and a starred canton,
  simplified).
- The standing figure: simplified, dignified form with headscarf, a cloth
  wrap skirt as drawn, a cloth band across the torso, arms lowered and
  crossed in front, hands chained (chains run down and across to the
  column), and an ankle shackle on her right ankle.

**Figure treatment (deliberate).** The figure is a simplified record of
the composition. The original engraving uses the exaggerated features of
period *Punch* caricature; those are NOT reproduced — the head and body
are plain, dignified, mannequin-like forms with no facial features. The
engraving bares the figure's torso; this model adds a plain cloth wrap
band across the torso as a modern, dignified simplification. The dark
bronze material follows the print's dark tonal rendering of the imagined
statue; the print is a cartoon of a proposed statue, so no real object's
material exists to match.

**Scale.** ESTIMATED. The print depicts an imagined monument: figure
≈ 1.58 m, pedestal drum Ø ≈ 0.95 m, total height ≈ 2.55 m. Proportions
follow the engraving; absolute scale is a modelling choice, marked
estimated.

**Assumptions and unseen sides.** The engraving shows one front
three-quarter view. The pedestal's rear ornament (assumed: the swag
pattern does not continue), the column's back, the flag's reverse and
the figure's back are interpreted minimally. Motto letterforms follow
the print's Roman capitals, front arc only.

**Counts / QC.** 28,464 triangles (Model collection, evaluated). Preview
rendered from a front three-quarter angle comparable to the engraving,
neutral grey backdrop, Cycles CPU.
