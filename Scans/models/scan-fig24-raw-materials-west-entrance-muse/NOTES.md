# scan-fig24-raw-materials-west-entrance — Raw Materials outside the West Entrance, 1851

**Source:** `fig-24_raw-materials-west-entrance-crystal-palace.png` (catalog fig. 24, scan IMG_6461).
Colour view of the outdoor raw-materials display beside the Palace.

## Subject
The open-air exhibit as shown: a tall tapered stone obelisk (the centrepiece),
large leaning slate slabs to its left, rough sandstone boulders in the foreground,
stacks and leaning sheets of pale stone slabs, leaning timber baulks, a stack of black
slate slabs, a wooden lattice hoist tower with pyramidal cap, a fluted column shaft on
a base, and the small distant building with a round-window frieze visible in the view.
Behind/right, a backdrop slice of the Palace's west front: three bays of iron-and-glass
frontage (columns, tier band, mullions, glass). Four stylised visitors, one holding a
sheet of paper as in the view — interpretive figures.

## Scale
Estimated from the figures in the view (≈1.7 m): obelisk ≈8.6 m tall, hoist tower ≈7.7 m
to cap, slabs 2–3.4 m. **Scale class: estimated.** The backdrop frontage reuses the
documented 24 ft (7.315 m) palace bay rhythm, compressed to 0.62× along its run so the
slice fits the vignette — flagged here, not presented as the true bay spacing.

## Assumptions / uncertainties
- Materials are identified from colour and context (slate, sandstone, marble shaft);
  individual stone types are not labelled in the view and are approximate.
- The Palace front is a backdrop slice only — two tiers suggested, not the full
  west-end elevation.
- The distant equestrian statue visible in the original is omitted (background detail,
  outside the display itself); the small frieze building is heavily simplified.
- The far right edge of the scan meets the photograph boundary; nothing was invented
  beyond it.
