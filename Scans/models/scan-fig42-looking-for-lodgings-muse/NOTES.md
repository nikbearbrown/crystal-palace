# scan-fig42-looking-for-lodgings — Muse version

**Muse version** — built by Muse for the Crystal Palace film project (Scans batch 6).

## Subject
George Cruikshank, "Looking for lodgings" (linked in the source book to Henry
Mayhew's *Mr. and Mrs. Sandboys*): a respectable family is shown a cramped
cellar lodging. Reference: `fig-42_george-cruikshank-looking-for-lodgings.png`
(scan IMG_6448) and its catalog description — "A family is shown a cramped
cellar lodging containing a bed and hammock."

## What is modelled
A composed interior vignette (~5.2 m wide) following the engraving's
arrangement:
- Brick cellar room with a low barrel-vaulted ceiling (vault profile derived
  from the arc visible in the print), stone-flag floor, small framed picture.
- Arched doorway in the left wall, raised on a step, door ajar.
- The hammock slung diagonally across the upper room from a ceiling hook —
  the print's dominant feature — with gathered rope ends and dark bedding.
- Furniture as drawn: small table and chair at centre, two low stools in the
  foreground, a simple bed along the back-right wall, and the barrel
  washstand with jug and basin at right.
- Five stylised figures: the tall visitor ducking through the doorway, a
  bonneted woman behind him, a cloaked man with a cane, a woman holding a
  folded umbrella, and the landlady holding a candlestick with one arm raised
  toward the hammock.

## Scale
Estimated. Room width 5.2 m, vault crown ~3.1 m, door figures ~1.8 m —
consistent with a cramped London cellar lodging and the relative proportions
in the print. No measured source exists for this fictional interior; treat
all dimensions as an interpretive estimate.

## Assumptions / interpretation (not confirmed by the single view)
- The print shows one viewpoint only. The room's depth (3.8 m), the right and
  rear wall extents, and the bed's exact position along the back wall are
  reasonable period interpretations, not evidence.
- The vault is drawn as a plain brick barrel vault; the print's hatching does
  not resolve its exact profile, springing height, or material finish.
- Figures are stylised silhouettes in period dress — poses and grouping follow
  the print, but faces, likenesses and costume detail are not reproduced.
- The landlady's candlestick is given a small lit flame as a lighting accent;
  the print does not unambiguously show the candle lit.
- No lettering or text is modelled anywhere.

## Files / QC
- `scan-fig42-looking-for-lodgings.blend` (metres, origin at base centre of
  the room floor, scale applied), `.glb` export, `preview.png` rendered from
  a viewpoint comparable to the engraving (front, slightly above eye level).
- Triangles: 9,060 (budget < 50k).
- QC: geometry built procedurally from named primitives; one object per
  figure/furniture item, grouped under a single Model collection.

QC note (final): preview art-directed over several passes — figures re-faced toward the landlady, back wall raised to seal against the vault (an early pass leaked background through the slot above the wall), a stage-flat header closes the vault mouth at frame top, bedding nested into the hammock sag. Residual: the hammock reads as a shallow slung band rather than the print's deep cocoon; figures are mannequin-stylised by design.
