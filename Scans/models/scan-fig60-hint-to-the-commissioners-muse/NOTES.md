# scan-fig60-hint-to-the-commissioners — Muse version

**This is the Muse version** of this model (ships as `scan-fig60-hint-to-the-commissioners-muse`).

## Source
- Figure 60, John Leech, "A Hint to the Commissioners", *Punch*, 26 April 1851.
- Source image: `fig-60_john-leech-hint-to-the-commissioners-1851.png` (catalogue scan IMG_6435).
- The cartoon's caption has two French visitors asking what "cette machine-là" is, in front of
  an ordinary washstand — *Punch*'s "hint" being that exhibits needed labels. One view only.

## Subject modelled
A composed outdoor vignette (~6 m wide) following the drawing's arrangement:
- **The washstand (the "machine")**, centre-right: four-legged table with two drawers, a
  raised back board carrying two **blank** card panels, two ewer-and-basin sets on top
  (one white, one blue ceramic — lathe-turned ewers with handles and spouts), a large slop
  basin and a jug on the lower shelf, and a stave bucket with iron bands holding a lidded
  jug at its right foot.
- **Three visitors** at left, turned toward the stand: a short man in a long patterned
  dressing-gown robe with a wide collar and wide-brimmed hat; a tall bearded man in a top
  hat and checked suit with one hand raised to his chest; a man in a flat cap with a cream
  waistcoat and baggy trousers.
- **The attendant** at right in top hat and cream waistcoat, his near arm extended in an
  open explaining gesture toward the washstand.
- Setting: a large tree (trunk, two branches, sparse canopy masses) centre-back, and a
  low exhibition arcade wall with two arch insets at back right, as drawn.

## Treatment of the source
The original is a Francophobic joke at the visitors' expense. The composition and the
washstand are preserved; the figures are dignified stylised forms in period dress with no
facial features, no likenesses, and none of Leech's caricature exaggeration. No lettering:
the washstand's cards are blank (fittingly, the missing labels are the joke's point, but no
text is invented for them).

## Scale
- **Estimated** from the figures (adults 1.72–1.80 m). Washstand 1.16 m wide, top at
  0.85 m, back board to ~1.24 m — typical of a bedroom washstand of the period.
  Vignette footprint 6.6 × 3.6 m.

## Assumptions and interpretation
- The washstand's rear and underside, and the ewers' far sides, are period-typical
  interpretation; the drawing shows one three-quarter view.
- The robe's pattern is suggested by plain vertical striping only.
- The checked suit is rendered in a plain check-toned cloth (the check itself is not
  woven into the geometry).
- The arcade wall is a hint, not a reconstruction of any specific part of the building;
  the crowd figures in the drawing's middle distance are omitted.
- Tree canopy is three flattened masses — a backdrop device, not a botanical study.
