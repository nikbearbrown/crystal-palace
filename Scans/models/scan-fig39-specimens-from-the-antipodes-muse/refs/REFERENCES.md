## What was taken from the view

- The composition: a row of arched, glass-fronted display alcoves against a dark interior; inside them, labelled "specimens" of labour — a seated needlewoman sewing (left), a bent elderly labourer in a pointed nightcap with a hand tool (centre), and a seated shoemaker at work among shoe forms (right).
- In front of the cases, two top-hatted gentlemen viewers: one tall and thin with his hand raised to his chin, one portly with a watch chain and cane.

## What was NOT taken from the view

- The case labels visible in the print ("An industrious needle-woman", "A labourer aged 75", "A distressed shoemaker", "A sweater") are **not** reproduced: the placards in the model are blank boards, per the no-lettering rule. The satire is carried by the cases themselves.
- No facial features or likenesses; the workers are dignified stylised figures, not caricatures.
- The backs and unseen sides (including case interiors beyond the front view) are reasonable interpretations, flagged in NOTES.md.
