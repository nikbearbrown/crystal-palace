# NOTES — scan-fig06-brunel-portrait (Muse version)

**Source:** fig-06_isambard-kingdom-brunel-robert-howlett-1857.png —
Robert Howlett's 1857 photograph of Isambard Kingdom Brunel standing
before the launching chains of the Great Eastern (catalog fig. 6,
IMG_6425).

## Subject
A stylised standing figure in 1851–57 gentleman's dress — dark frock
coat, waistcoat with watch chain and fob, white shirt front, cravat,
trousers, and the tall stovepipe hat worn ON the head as photographed —
posed hands-in-pockets against a wall of massive stud-link anchor
chain, with two loose links at the base. The figure is a mannequin-like
record of pose and dress: **no facial features and no likeness are
modelled**, per the batch rule for figures.

## Scale
Figure ≈ 1.70 m overall (1.97 m to the hat crown), per the brief's
life-size figure scale. **Manual-review note:** Brunel is historically
reported to have been short (about 1.52–1.57 m); the brief's ~1.7 m
was used — flag if a true-to-Brunel stature is wanted instead. Chain
links ≈ 0.56 m long, sized from their proportion to the figure in the
photograph (the Great Eastern's chain links were of this order);
**estimated**, not measured from a dimensioned source.

## Assumptions / interpretation
- Single frontal photograph: the figure's back, the coat tails and the
  chain wall's depth are period interpretation.
- The chains are shown as a flat hanging wall of interlocked stud
  links as they read in the photograph; their real arrangement on the
  slipway (piled/draped) is simplified.
- The small white cylinder at the mouth records the cigar visible in
  the photograph as a pose detail.
- The print's rounded top corners are a print artefact and are NOT
  modelled (catalog uncertainty noted and set aside, as briefed).
- Materials are flat period tones (charcoal coat, iron chain); the
  photograph is monochrome, so all colours are conventional.
