## What was taken from the view

- The composition: the Crystal Palace at centre-back (glazed nave, raised central transept with a great arch, a tall tower and chimney behind the roof, a banner board across the roof front), with the exhibits scattering outward in every direction — a locomotive, a horse-drawn carriage, boats under sail, a great fish, a polar bear, a globe, furniture, a cannon, a striped lighthouse, a statue, a great urn, barrels and crates — and visitors streaming away among them, some carrying bundles and portmanteaus.
- The palace is a simplified "hint" of the building, at the modest scale the print gives it relative to the exhibits.
- Relative sizes are read loosely from a deliberately fantastical print (basis of the estimated scale — see NOTES.md).

## What was NOT taken from the view

- The roof banner's lettering in the print is **not** reproduced: the board is blank, per the no-lettering rule.
- The print's smallest background clutter (dozens of minor objects, animals and figures) is reduced to a representative selection; see NOTES.md.
- Nothing in the print is to scale — it is a satire — so every dimension in the model is an interpretation, flagged in NOTES.md.
- Elevated pieces (boats, ship, fish, urn) are held on slim dark display struts, a staging device to suggest the drawn flight; the print of course shows them unsupported in the air.
