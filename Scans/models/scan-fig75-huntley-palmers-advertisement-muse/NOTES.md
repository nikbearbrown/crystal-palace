# NOTES — scan-fig75-huntley-palmers-advertisement (Muse version)

**Subject.** Huntley & Palmers centenary advertisement, Illustrated London
News, 12 May 1951 (fig-75 of the Scans catalog): a line-drawn composition
in which two women in 1851 dress walk across grass toward the Crystal
Palace while an oversized Huntley & Palmers biscuit tin stands in the
left foreground. The model restages that composition as a small still-life
vignette. This package is the Muse version of the subject, built for the
Crystal Palace film project.

**What was modelled**
- The giant biscuit tin (foreground, image-left as drawn): cream enamelled
  rectangular tin with a stepped lid, a blank gold label disc on the lid,
  and label bands on the front and side — red top band, navy bottom band,
  pale label panel with a pale-blue oval (the palace vignette printed on
  the real label, abstracted). The brand name and all other lettering on
  the tin are NOT reproduced; the bands are left blank deliberately.
- Two visitors seen from behind, walking toward the palace: stylised
  figures in 1851 dress silhouettes (crinoline dresses, dark bonnets;
  one carries a small basket). No facial features, no likenesses.
- The palace as a flat backdrop elevation in the drawing's manner:
  stepped nave wings with mullion strips and arch arcs, central transept
  with concentric arches, the two great elms inside the transept, and
  flag poles with red flags above the transept, as drawn.
- A plain grass ground slab tying the vignette together.

**Scale.** ESTIMATED throughout. The tin is modelled at its advertising
scale in the drawing (~1.5 m wide, roughly 3–4× a real biscuit tin)
relative to the women (~1.65 m); the palace backdrop is a compressed
elevation ~17 m wide, not the building's true size — the vignette follows
the drawing's internal proportions, not real-world ones.

**Assumptions and unseen sides.**
- The source is a monochrome line drawing; all colours (dress colours,
  tin enamels, grass, the palace's glass tone) are interpretive.
- The tin's rear and the label's printed scene are not visible in the
  drawing; the rear is plain and the label oval is a blank abstraction.
- The palace is a flat elevation only — it has no modelled depth, roof
  structure or interior, matching its role as the drawing's backdrop.
- The figures' faces are turned away in the source; the models keep them
  featureless from all sides.

**Counts / QC.** 6,192 triangles (Model collection, evaluated). Preview
rendered from behind the visitors at an angle comparable to the drawing,
neutral grey backdrop, Cycles CPU, 48 samples + denoise. QC fix during
the build: the first framing cropped the tin and the palace ends and the
transept elms were oversized — camera pulled back/widened and the elms
reduced; final preview verified against the drawing's composition (tin
lower-left, women centre, palace behind).

**Files.** `scan-fig75-huntley-palmers-advertisement.blend`,
`scan-fig75-huntley-palmers-advertisement.glb`, `preview.png`,
`build_scan_fig75_huntley_palmers.py` (re-runnable).

**Manual review.** Recommended: (1) the composition is a deliberate

monochrome).
