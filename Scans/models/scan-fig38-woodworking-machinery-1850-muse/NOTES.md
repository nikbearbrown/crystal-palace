**Muse version.** Staged locally as `scan-fig38-woodworking-machinery-1850/`; ships as `scan-fig38-woodworking-machinery-1850-muse/` under Scans/models/.

- **Source:** `fig-38_workers-building-crystal-palace-1850.png` (scan IMG_6451), ILN 7 Dec 1850, p.132. Catalog description: "Engraving of workers operating woodworking machinery during construction." Catalog lists no uncertainties; repair: none.
- **Subject modelled:** the construction workshop's planing machine as principal subject: heavy timber planing bench running to the machine with planks on it, tapered cast-iron machine housing on a block base, the large pale circular cutter disc with three radial slots (the engraving's most distinctive feature), arched belt guard on top, and the line shaft extending right on timber posts with pulleys and belt runs. The long operating lever angles down in front of the machine. Supporting set: timber plank stack, offcuts at the machine base, a plain background bench with stacked boards. Three stylised workers (feeder with apron at the bench, lever operator, plank handler) — the machines, not the men, are the subject.
- **Scale:** ESTIMATED from the workers (~1.70–1.74 m). Cutter disc Ø ~0.84 m as drawn against the feeder's torso; bench top at 0.90 m.
- **Assumptions / interpretation:** the engraving is the only view; the machine's cutter-head geometry behind the disc, its drive source (off-frame), and the housing's rear form are period-typical interpretation. The two background planers visible in the engraving are represented by their bench and boards only, keeping the figure count at the briefed three and the emphasis on the machinery. The bare tree and building frame in the engraving's background are not modelled. Figures are simplified period-dress forms — no likenesses.
