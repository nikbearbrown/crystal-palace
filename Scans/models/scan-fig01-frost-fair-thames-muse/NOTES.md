# scan-fig01-frost-fair-thames — Muse version

**Muse version** — built by Muse for the Crystal Palace film project (Scans batch 6).

## Subject
"Fair on the Thames, 1683" — the frost fair held on the frozen river during
the Great Frost. Reference: `fig-01_fair-on-the-thames-1683.png` (scan
IMG_6422) and its catalog description — "Historical engraving of a fair on
the frozen Thames, with booths, crowds and activities on the river."

## What is modelled
A composed panorama vignette 30 m wide x 20 m deep, following the
engraving's layout:
- A flat ice plane with snow patches and hairline cracks.
- Two rows of fair booths (eleven in total): timber counters and back boards,
  sloped striped awnings (red/white), draped curtain fronts, goods on the
  counters, flag poles with pennants — the booths fill the middle band of the
  print.
- Crowd groupings as drawn: two rings of onlookers on the open ice behind
  the booths (13 and 15 figures, plus two performers in the right ring), a
  whirligig horse walking its circle by a fenced pit at centre-back,
  scattered skaters with outspread arms in the foreground, and walking
  figures in the booth street.
- Two horse-drawn coaches driving on the foreground ice (one pair-horse,
  one single) and a third, horseless coach standing on the far ice at upper
  right (print no. 28); a sail-wheeled boat at lower left, a larger sailing
  barge at lower right with a four-man hauling crew, and a horse-drawn
  covered sledge at foreground centre.
- The backdrop is a flat silhouette strip, as the brief specifies: old London
  Bridge with its piers and the houses built along it, the White Tower of the
  Tower of London, a church spire, the Monument column, Southwark's tower,
  rooftop blocks and bare trees at both ends.

## Scale
Estimated. Booths ~2.4 m wide / ~2.5 m to the awning ridge (per the brief's
~2.5 m booth assumption), figures ~1.55–1.75 m, coaches ~4.5 m overall with
horses. The real frost fair stretched across the whole river; this is a
compressed stage vignette, not a measured reconstruction.

## Assumptions / interpretation (not confirmed by the single view)
- The engraving is a schematic broadside, not a measured view: booth counts,
  spacing, the rings of figures and the positions of coaches are composed to
  match its arrangement, not surveyed fact.
- Booth construction (timber counter, canvas curtain, striped awning) is the
  standard frost-fair booth form as drawn; rear and side elevations are
  interpreted — the print shows fronts only.
- The skyline is deliberately a flat backdrop silhouette, not a 3D model of
  the City or the bridge; individual buildings are generic period forms
  keyed to the print's shapes (Tower, Monument, spires).
- Figures are stylised silhouettes in 17th-century dress (broad-brim hats,
  cloaks, bonnets) — no likenesses; activities (skating, watching) are
  suggested by pose only.
- The numbered keys printed in the engraving (its legend references) are not
  reproduced.

## Files / QC
- `scan-fig01-frost-fair-thames.blend` (metres, origin at base centre of the
  ice plane, scale applied), `.glb` export, `preview.png` from an elevated
  front viewpoint comparable to the engraving.
- Triangles: 47,490 (budget < 50k).
- QC note: preview art-directed over several passes — an early pass had
  the coaches and horses sunk into the ice (a join-origin fault, fixed with
  root parts in every joined builder) and a framing that cropped the
  foreground craft; the final framing is the elevated wide view of the
  print. The backdrop silhouette shows a few very dark slots between piers
  and rooftops (deep shade in the flat silhouette material) — read as the
  print's black skyline accents.
- QC: procedural build; booths, coaches, horses and figures are joined
  single meshes, grouped under one Model collection.
